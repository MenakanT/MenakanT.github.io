Hack Western 13 · QNX track · 2026

Super Safety Hat

A construction hard hat with a rear-facing camera and a microphone that watches and listens behind the wearer. Two TensorFlow Lite models run on a Raspberry Pi 5 running QNX Neutrino 8.0: YAMNet classifies sounds like horns, sirens, reversing beepers and shouts, and EfficientDet-Lite0 detects people and vehicles. A tracker estimates time to contact and spots a person falling. Audio and vision are fused into spoken warnings like “Horn and truck behind you on your left, about 2 seconds away,” and every detection feeds a site-wide dashboard on Tiger Cloud (TimescaleDB).

Results: All inference runs on the hat with no network needed. Sound classification takes about 6 ms per window and object detection about 55 ms per frame on the Pi 5.

My role: Main programmer and Raspberry Pi lead. Some of what I worked on:

  • Running both AI models on the Pi under QNX
  • Webcam capture through the QNX camera service
  • Detection, tracking and alert logic
  • The SSH link between the Pi and the laptop
  • Python
  • Raspberry Pi 5
  • QNX
  • TensorFlow Lite
  • YAMNet
  • EfficientDet
  • TimescaleDB

Personal project · 2026

Vision target tracker

A detection pipeline that finds objects in video and outputs each one’s class, confidence and pixel centroid (u, v), the first step toward robotic grasping and visual servoing. I sampled frames from my own footage, labelled them and trained a YOLO11n model in Roboflow, then wrote an OpenCV pipeline that runs every frame through it and draws boxes, labels and centroids.

Personal project

Two-axis smartphone gimbal

A custom stabilizer that cut visible shake by 60% in walking tests. An ESP32 reads an MPU6050 and runs a 200 Hz PID loop with under 10 ms end-to-end latency. The printed frame weighs under 250 g and costs under $5 in material, and a custom battery pack and buck-boost converter give over 3 hours of use.
